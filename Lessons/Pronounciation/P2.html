PRONUNCIATION : SILENT LETTERS AND LIAISON
Silent Letters

French has letters that in some situations, can be silent, meaning that they won't be pronounced at all

Note : Depending on the region, some letters can be pronounced even if they are supposed to be silent.

The Silent E :

The letter E is silent when placed at the end of a word :

une Salade (a Salad)

une Carotte (a carrot)

The Silent H :

The letter H is, most of the time, silent. The only situations in which it is not silent are when it is used with "c" or "p" : "ch" /ʃ/ ; "ph" /f/

un Hôtel (a Hotel)

une Hache (an Axe)

Silent consonants at the end of words (t, s, x, d)

The consonants t, s, x, d are most of the time siletn when placed at the end of a word.

Warning : There are excpetions

un lit (a bed)

un radis (a radish)

une croix (a cross)

un renard (a fox)
The "Liaison"

General Definition : When a word end by a consonant, and the next word starts by a vowel, this consonant is pronounced

Here's a table of what sound these liaison make :

Letter Sound Example
D /t/ Un grand arbre (a great tree)
F /v/ neuf ans (nine years)
T /t/ tout à fait (absolutely)
G /g/ un long hiver (a long winter)
N /n/ un ancien ami (an old friend)
P /p/ Beacoup à faire (a lot to do)
R /ʁ/ Le premier étage (the first floor)
S /z/ les amis (the friends)
X /z/ deux ans (two years)
Z /z/ chez un ami (at a friend's place)

However, rules exist about liaison. There are 3 situations :

  • Required "liaison"
  • Optional "liaison"
  • Forbidden "liaison"

Required Liaisons :

Situation Example
In a Nominal Group (Article + noun / adjective + Noun) mes vieux oncles allemands (my old german uncles)
In a Nominal Group (Number + noun / adjective) Trois amis (three friends)
In a Verbal Group (Pronoun + Pronoun / Pronoun + verb) Il nous en informe (he's informing us about it)
Single syllable adverbs, conjunbction, or prepositions très étrange (very strange) (adverb); Mais encore ? (conjunction); chez un ami (preposition)
Fixed expressions C'est à dire (in other words); plus ou moins (approximately, kind of)

Optional Liaisons :

Situation Example
After plural nouns les livres incroyables (the incredible books)
Between the two parts of a verb conjugated at any compound tense Ils ont eu (they had / they've had)
Between "être" and an adjective, an adverb or a noun Il est étonné (He's surprised) ; Je suis ici (I'm here) ; Il est acteur (he's an actor)
After multi-syllable adverbs and prepositions après avoir mangé (after having eaten) ;
